Part 1(b) — solution

Discuss the strengths of the brand identity created through the Cook 9 Islands’ ‘Love a little paradise’ logo. Candidates are expected to be familiar with characteristics of an effective brand identity. They should be able to combine their theoretical knowledge of these concepts with information from Fig.1.1, to present an applied response specific to the context of the Cook Islands. Indicative content: Brand name – clear, concise Slogan/tagline – Love a little paradise – association with paradise consistent with island destination messages; Cultural association – national flower Text in the logo is clear, some decorative elements, USP – colour here, use of the word paradise Use of colour – shocking pink is eye-catching Price in association with image – not referenced Distinctive packaging – not referenced Corporate identity (uniforms, furnishings, etc.) – not referenced Other relevant information should also be credited. Mark according to the levels of response criteria below. Level 3 (7–9 marks) Candidates provide a discussion of the strengths of the characteristics of brand identity of Cook Island’s brand identity. There is sound and frequent evidence of thorough, detailed and accurate knowledge and understanding of concepts and principles, using appropriate terminology as well as specific reference to the Cook Islands. Level 2 (4–6 marks) Candidates provide an explanation of the strengths of the brand identity of the Cook Islands. Candidates will show an understanding of the question and include identification and explanation of one, two or more characteristics. The answer is relevant and shows reasonable knowledge and understanding of specific marketing concepts and principles, with some use of appropriate terminology and some reference to the Cook Islands. Level 1 (1–3 marks) Candidates identify/describe up to three strengths of establishing the brand identity but may not refer to the Cook Islands. The answer shows limited knowledge and understanding of specific marketing concepts and principles with limited use of appropriate terminology. Part 2(b) — solution

Assess the likely benefits to Toscana Promozione Turistica (TPT) and 9 Destination Florence Convention and Visitors Bureau (DFCVB) of working in partnership to promote the Tuscany MICE brand. Candidates should be familiar with the interdependency of stakeholders from the syllabus. They should use this knowledge to apply to the context of this question. Indicative content: Shared promotion / promotional costs / extension of the marketing budget Pooling of expertise – sharing specialisms Larger customer base Bigger reach from regional base Added value for the customer – brand is perceived as more reliable because of the endorsement of a partner organisation Increased exposure, networking and connections Other relevant information should also be credited. Mark according to the levels of response criteria below. Level 3 (7–9 marks) Candidates provide an assessment of the benefits of partnership working in Tuscany. There is sound and frequent evidence of thorough, detailed and accurate knowledge and understanding of concepts and principles, using appropriate terminology as well as specific reference to the Tuscany. Level 2 (4–6 marks) Candidates provide an explanation of the benefits of partnership working in Tuscany. Candidates will show an understanding of the question and include identification and explanation of one, two or more benefits. The answer is relevant and shows reasonable knowledge and understanding of specific marketing concepts and principles, with some use of appropriate terminology and some reference to the Tuscany. Level 1 (1–3 marks) Candidates identify/describe up to three benefits of partnership working in Tuscany. The answer shows limited knowledge and understanding of specific marketing concepts and principles with limited use of appropriate terminology. There may be little reference to Tuscany. Candidates should be familiar with the range of marketing activities carried out by tourism organisations. Indicative content: Trade fairs and roadshows are an international example of marketing activity They raise awareness – through face to face contact They are targeted at the travel trade – industry specific Increases the product knowledge of personnel who will be involved in using the services of the trade Networking opportunities – building contacts Launch – allows opportunity for PR exercise of introducing the new brand Other relevant information should also be credited.
